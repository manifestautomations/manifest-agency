# The Death of the Retainer

If you hire a lawyer, you pay for their time. If they spend 10 hours on your case, you pay for 10 hours. If they are slow, meticulous, or just bad at typing, you pay more.

For decades, software agencies worked the same way. "Time and Materials." You buy a bucket of 100 developer hours, and we promise to fill them with code.

**But then the robots arrived.**

## The AI Paradox

Today, I can generate a landing page, a database schema, and an API endpoint in the time it takes to brew a coffee.

If I were billing you by the hour, I would be *punished* for this efficiency.
- **The Old Way:** 40 hours @ $150/hr = $6,000 revenue.
- **The AI Way:** 2 hours @ $150/hr = $300 revenue.

If I use AI to be 20x faster, I make 20x less money. This is a broken incentive structure. It encourages agencies to ban AI, work slowly, and pad their timesheets.

We refuse to play that game.

## Enter: Earned Autonomy

At Manifest, we don't sell hours. We sell **velocity**.

Our model is simple:
1.  **Results-Based Pricing:** You pay a flat monthly subscription.
2.  **Outcome-Focused:** We agree on deliverables (Features, Fixes, Deployments).
3.  **Aligned Incentives:** If we use AI to finish your project in 3 days instead of 3 weeks, *you get your product faster* and *we maximize our margin*. Everyone wins.

### Why "Earned" Autonomy?

We start small. We don't ask for a 12-month contract on day one.
We act as an extension of your team.
- **Level 1 (Intern):** We pick up small tickets. You review every line.
- **Level 2 (Junior):** We handle features. You review the PRs.
- **Level 3 (Partner):** We have merge rights. We deploy. You sleep.

We earn your trust through shipping, not through timesheets.

## The Future of Work

The agencies that cling to hourly billing are walking dead. They just don't know it yet. Clients will stop tolerating "2 weeks for a button change" when they know it can be done in 2 seconds.

We are building the agency of the future. It’s faster, it’s leaner, and it doesn't care how many hours we sat in a chair.

It only cares about one thing: **Did we ship?**
